# Inworld VIBE: metrics, views and diagnostics

The detail behind the [leaderboard](README.md): the two boards and the views of each, the task contracts, the conditions of the set, how every axis is measured, the diagnostics reported beside the boards and how a score is bound to what was rendered.
This page defines what each view measures; the tables are generated by `scripts/leaderboard.py` and the sidecars from score files that are not distributed.

## Views of the boards

### Two boards: denoising and restoration

VIBE publishes two scores per system, each on its own board, and no composite of both.
Denoising is the mean of three parts, what a system removes (suppression, music, cleanup, events, voices), what it keeps (preservation, fidelity) and how the result sounds (timbre, quality); it reads the denoise contract, and it ranks the systems that add nothing above the input's band.
Restoration is the fourth part alone, what a system restores: the room axis, the dry voice out of a real room; it ranks the systems that restore above the input's band (more than 10 dB on the band-limited rows).
Every system is scored on both boards and the board `leaderboard.py --json-out` writes carries both scores; its tables list each system under the board that ranks it.
The rule is the registry's (`axes.json`, `assign`): a system without the band-limited rows is ranked on neither board and listed as not ranked, since missing evidence is not a kept band, and the restoration board ranks the full-band tier, so a 16 kHz-tier system the rule sends there is listed as not ranked too.
Each score needs its own axes and no other's, so a system complete on denoising ranks there whatever its restoration rows hold.
The band axis is scored and shown beside both scores but included in neither.
It uses the two band-limited rows whose input lacks the most, `srsweep_8k` (a 4 kHz band) and `srsweep_16k` (8 kHz), with two measurements each:

- `band_added_db`: the energy the output holds above the input's band, against the reference band-limited the same way, less the mean of the bands inside it, so a gain applied everywhere cancels. It is derived from the scorer's per-band levels at load (and per item by the comparison tools) and scaled from the input's own −0.85 dB to a declared 40 dB, since the set holds no reference above the input's band.
- `spk_sim`: the speaker's similarity to that reference.

It credits any addition above the band, a tone included, and the speaker row, read at 16 kHz, cannot see what sits above 8 kHz, so the pair measures addition and not the restoration of speech.
It stays a diagnostic until a measure of the reconstructed band's speech exists; the systems that add above the band are ranked on the restoration board, so a denoising reader meets none of them in the denoising table.
The `*` after a system's name marks streaming: the streaming probe is yes.
The script also prints the order under a strict deleted-rate gate.
A row whose level calibration saturated is not ranked, and the script says why.

### Raw diagnostics

The tables print raw measurements beside the axes; higher is better for the normalized columns, and the raw ones read as follows.
More negative `supp dB` means less interference left under crowd chatter at the voice's scale.
More negative `pauses dB` means the real recordings' pauses sit further below the output's own voice (capped at −45).
Lower `del %` means fewer deleted primary-speaker segments.
Lower `laughs lost %` means fewer of the vocal sounds outside speech lost under a bed at the voice's level.
Higher `NISQA` means greater predicted listening quality.
`precision` is the arithmetic each wrapper runs its network in, declared beside its receipts rather than measured.
Each row names how the system was run where that matters (`wrappers/meta/<name>.json`).
The two URGENT 2026 baselines say whether the release script's final peak normalization is kept ([WRAPPER_CONTRACT.md](WRAPPER_CONTRACT.md)).

### Core view
A `*` after a system's name marks streaming (the streaming probe is yes); the systems that restore above the input's band are listed under Restoration and the others under Denoising, as on the whole-set board.

The set has a multilingual core, where English carries about half of every paired condition and eleven other languages share the rest, and an English-only extension of extra items and the families that exist only in English.
The leaderboard in [README.md](README.md) ranks on everything.
This one ranks the same systems on the core alone, scored separately (`BENCH_SUBSET=core`, `results/core/`) on the core axes of the registry (`axes.json`), so the extension cannot move the multilingual result.
Those axes leave out the rows of the English-only families, the −5 dB rows of stationary noise and traffic among them; crowd chatter at −5 dB, which the core's recordings carry, is read as on the whole set.
The recordings of laughs, breaths and sighs exist in English only, so the core has no such rows and no `laughs lost %`.

`scripts/leaderboard.py --view core --results <results>/core` prints both boards of the core.

**How to read it.**
Each score is a stated choice: the mean of its parts, fixed anchors, no hard gate, and a gap under 0.03 is a tie (the script prints every group of tied systems).
The timbre axis stretches a twentieth of the speaker-similarity scale over its whole length, so a timbre gap under 0.05 is a tie as well.
Quote the axis columns, not the rank.
Per-item delay alignment cannot undo phase changes or time-varying changes from vocoder re-synthesis.
Waveform-coherence and residual measurements can therefore penalize a natural-sounding re-synthesis without establishing that words were lost.
For those systems, also read NISQA, speaker similarity, primary-speaker deletion, downstream ASR and the content check, and listen; the composite alone does not settle the comparison.
A system is ranked only when every axis of the board that ranks it has at least 20 scored items per condition and its primary-anchored deletion sidecar covers the complete frozen INPUT cohort with unchanged segment denominators.
All rows are measured on that cohort, independent of which competitors are present.
A system that failed to return some items is listed under *missing outputs*.

### Competing voices: the voices axis

The voices axis reads SI-SDR on the competing-talker condition at +5 dB against the one voice the recording is of, scaled from the mixture's own 5 dB to the 20 dB ceiling the other SI-SDR rows use.
It belongs to the removes part: the task contract makes the competing talker noise to remove.
`scripts/leaderboard.py --outside-out <file> --axis-tables voices` writes the axis alone, in its own order.

### Real rooms

The room axis: speech recorded in real rooms, scored against the dry voice by speaker similarity and predicted quality.
A system that removes the room scores high; one that leaves it as recorded, or does worse than the recording, scores zero: a zero is not by itself a passthrough.
The axis is the restores part on its own: it is the restoration score.

`scripts/leaderboard.py --outside-out <file> --axis-tables room` writes the axis alone, in its own order.

### Streaming probe

The `streaming probe` column is read from a probe, not from a model's description, so it measures the inference path a system actually ships, whatever its description says.
The probe takes every paired 45 s item of the set and cuts four inputs from each: the fifteen seconds from 28.8 to 43.8 s with the 3.2 s before them as a lead-in, on their own, and the same cut whose audio from a splice point on is another recording, at three splice points.
The cut starts on a 3.2 s grid, a multiple of every common frame hop, so the fresh render's frames fall where the whole render's did and the two differ by context alone; the lead-in lets a model's state warm up before the measured stretch.
The splice points are not round numbers, so a processor that works on independent blocks of some common length cannot be lucky on every splice.
Look-ahead is how far before a splice the two outputs already differ, at its worst over the three splices: a causal system reads its frame and look-ahead, an offline one reaches the start of the cut and is shown as at least that.
Drift compares the published render of the whole item, on the fifteen seconds, with the render of the cut: a model that carries state should give the same answer after 28.8 s of context as after 3.2 s, and the SI-SDR difference against the reference says whether it did.
Dropped speech counts the seconds where the reference speaks, the whole render sits 20 dB or more under it and the fresh render keeps it within 3 dB: speech the long context cost, not speech the system loses either way.
Yes needs all of it on the complete inventory of long items: a look-ahead of at most 100 ms on every item, a drift of at least −1 dB on average with no item under −5 dB, and no dropped second.
The scorer (`scripts/scorestream.py`) records every item's look-ahead, drift and dropped seconds under its own rule, which fixes how renders are read; the board (`scripts/streamview.py`) judges those measurements with the limits declared here, so a limit can move without reading a render again and every system is judged alike.
The thresholds are a screening policy, stated here, not a measure of delivery or throughput.
Reported beside the verdict and not gated: the cold start, how the first second of the cut compares with the same second inside the whole render (negative when the fresh start is worse; for a streaming model it reads its fresh state, for an offline one the edge of its input); and, for a release whose inference path depends on the input's length (a mark on the drift), that the whole item and the cut took different paths, so that difference includes the change of path.
The probe renders must come from the environment that rendered the board and from these probe inputs (a wrapper's receipts carry the digest the stamp records and a marker names the probe), every output must be exactly its input's length, and the whole render must be the audio that was scored; the leaderboard shows no verdict whose probe rule, scorer or render has since changed.

`scripts/leaderboard.py --streaming-out <file>` writes these measurements for every ranked system.
A † on the drift marks a release that takes different inference paths for the whole item and the cut (whole-file inference up to a length, segmented beyond), so that difference includes the change of path.

### How much of the order is policy

`scripts/anchorsweep.py --results <results> --markdown-out <file>` measures it on the full-band tier.
It draws every declared anchor constant inside its stated range (2000 seeded draws) and reports each system's denoising score between the 5th and 95th percentile and how often it ranks first, above the next system or tied with it; the raw measurements are the same in every draw, only the scale moves.
It then names every single constant whose range alone reverses a pair the declared board separates by more than the tie band of 0.03.
The other policy choice is how the axes combine; for it the sweep gives the order with one axis left out and how often each system ranks first when the weights of the axes are drawn uniformly at random.

## Task contracts

The set asks three different things, and a score is only meaningful under the contract it was measured against (`scripts/contracts.py` states them and what the axes may score; the leaderboard refuses any other row):

- **Denoise** (every row of the denoising board, and every condition not named below): the voice as recorded stays, its laughs, breaths and sighs with it, at its level, in
  its band and with its own room; everything that is not that voice goes, including noise that
  shares the voice's room and microphone (`coroom@*`, whose reference is the voice after both) and a
  competing talker (`talker@*`: the voice the recording is of stays, the other voice goes). `srsweep_*` belongs
  here: mixture and reference are band-limited identically, so the band the input lacks is not
  the system's to invent. A system that puts more than 10 dB more energy above the input's band
  than inside it, each against the reference band-limited the same way, restores, which this
  contract counts as invention: the restoration board ranks it, not the denoising board.
  Crowd chatter is read over the whole item at the voice's scale (the chatter left at each babble
  level, SI-SDR), so chatter under the speaker's own voice does move those rows; what no row
  separates is the chatter under speech from the chatter in the pauses and from distortion, so a
  difference in one of them is diluted across the denoising score. The competing talker itself is the
  **voices** axis of the removes part ([Competing voices](#competing-voices-the-voices-axis)).
- **Voice policy** (`singer@{-2,-5}`, `conversation@0`, `long@+4`, `music_vocal@{-6,+6}`; diagnostics
  outside both boards): which voices are content. The boards' own answer is the denoise contract
  above. These rows score the alternative policies: a singer who is the target stays, a song's own singer
  behind speech goes, both sides of a conversation stay, and so do the several talkers of a long item,
  who take turns. For a system that keeps one speaker, `conversation@0` and `long@+4` read how much of
  the other voices went, by design, not a failure. `long@+4` also feeds the streaming probe, which
  compares a system with itself.
- **Restoration** (`reverb_real`, the room axis; `reverb_synth`, diagnostic): the reference is
  better than the voice in the input, the dry voice out of a reverberant one. The room axis is
  the restoration board and nothing else is: a system that only removes what was added scores
  low there and keeps its denoising score. The band a narrow input lacks is shown beside
  both scores (`band`) and included in neither, since energy above the band is addition, not yet
  restoration.

## Metrics

- Conventions the scorer undoes before anything is read, because nobody can hear them: a delay of
  up to 100 ms and a flipped polarity (`lag_median`, `n_polarity_flipped` report them), and a
  system's overall gain of up to 12 dB (`_level_cal_db`, the median output-over-input level of
  the `clean` items). The gain is scored once, as the level change on clean speech, and undone
  everywhere else: in the kept energy and the lost blocks, in the suppression of a competing
  voice and of non-voiced sound, in the high-band error and in the deletion rule
  (`scripts/levels.py`). The bound matters: an output that is silent on clean speech cannot
  calibrate its deleted voice back.
  [CONTROLS.md](CONTROLS.md) feeds the scorer known damage and known non-damage and shows what
  every metric says about each, including what none of them sees.
- `pause_level_db`, `pause_resid_db` (`scoreresidual.py`, on the real recordings of every `real_*`
  family with at least 0.4 s of pause): what a system leaves in the input's own pauses, read at 48 kHz
  in one-third-octave bands weighted for audibility, with the output's delay and polarity undone and
  the first and last 300 ms of the item and 100/200 ms around every word left out. The level row reads
  it against the output's own voice, held within ±20 dB of the input's own pause level and capped at
  −45 dB where it is taken as inaudible, and feeds the cleanup axis;
  the reduction row reads it against what the recording held, each item's own gain undone, and is
  reported beside it. `babble_resid_db` (the same sidecar, on the crowd-chatter rows): the energy of
  the output that no scaling of the clean voice explains, at the voice's own scale, over the chatter
  the mixture held; interference and distortion together, so a quieter output earns nothing and loses
  nothing, and a vanished voice reads the worst value. Every item is held inside ±20 dB before
  pooling, so a few silenced pauses cannot carry an axis.
- `event_resid_db`, `floor_resid_db`, `voice_level_db` (`scoreevents.py`, on the same-room block).
  Each system renders the block twice, from the mixture and from the reference (the block's
  references are linked under `pair_coroom/in` beside the renders by `scoreevents.py --pair` and
  rendered the way the streaming probe is, in the environment that rendered the board). What the
  noise did to the output is the difference between the two renders, so neither row penalizes
  what a system does to the voice's own room.
  - `event_resid_db`, `floor_resid_db`: the energy of that difference over the energy the mixture
    added, in dB; the event row on each labeled event span plus 150 ms of the room's decay, the
    floor row on the labeled pauses outside the events. Every event is bounded before the item's
    mean.
  - `voice_level_db`: the voice's level on the reference's loudest fifth of speech frames, the
    lower of two comparisons: the mixture's render against the reference's render (what the noise
    costs the voice), and against the reference with the room's measured share allowed
    (`room_share_db` in the item's provenance: the room-free voice, level-matched, placed at the
    direct sound's arrival where a system that removes the room renders it). A system that turns
    the voice down, with or without the noise, is penalized; one that removes the room is not.
  - On both renders the system's delay and base gain are undone, a silent render takes the bound,
    and every item is held inside ±20 dB (the unbounded means stand beside the residual rows as
    `*_raw_db`).

  The event rows feed the events axis, the floor rows the cleanup axis and the voice level the
  preservation axis. The same sidecar reports, beside the board, the transients the shared
  detector finds in the real recordings outside speech (`_real_events`), a diagnostic because
  most of them are the speaker's own sounds.
- `si_sdr`: scale-invariant SDR against the reference.
- `kept_coh_mean`, `kept_coh_p10`: the voice kept, per 100 ms block over the reference's speech
  blocks (a voice-activity mask, so quiet speech counts), as the output's projection on the
  reference. An added bed projects to nothing, so returning the background cannot pass as kept
  speech, and a deleted quiet phrase shows as a large negative 10th percentile.
  `kept_coh_lost_pct` is the share of those blocks kept below −10 dB: a percentile cannot see one
  dropped syllable, a count of lost blocks can (reported, outside both scores). `kept_mean`,
  `kept_p10` are the plain energy ratios, reported beside them.
- `nv_lost_pct`, `nv_events_lost_pct` (`scorekept.py`, on the recordings of laughs, breaths and
  sighs): the voice outside speech. The blocks are those of the clean recording that hold sound
  (within 30 dB of its loudest block), that the voice-activity detector does not call speech,
  and that the bed does not bury (the voice of the block no more than 10 dB under the bed of the
  block). A block is lost by the rule of a speech block. The first is the share of blocks lost,
  pooled over the condition, and feeds the keep axis under a bed and the fidelity axis on clean
  recordings; the second reads runs of consecutive blocks as whole events and is reported beside
  it. The recordings are few and in English: 48 per condition, of which only those holding a
  block the rule can read observe the row (under the bed, fewer than half; a row needs 20).
- `spk_sim`: WavLM-SV cosine similarity to the reference. The timbre axis is its mean over
  twelve noise families, each at the level where the unprocessed mixture reads about 0.95
  (crowd chatter at +4 dB; ambience, events, cheering, stationary noise, appliances and tools at
  +5 dB; wind, traffic, household one-shots, animals and rain at 0 dB). Running water, mixed at
  0 dB only, leaves the mixture far under that scale and stays out.
- Room: on the recordings in real rooms, `spk_sim` and NISQA against the dry voice, from what
  the recording in its room reads to the dry reference's own.
- Streaming probe (`scorestream.py`, outside both scores): `look_ahead_ms`, the time before a
  splice in the input at which the output already differs (the first 10 ms block whose difference
  rises 60 dB above the output's floor), at its worst over three splices per item; `drift_db`,
  the SI-SDR of fifteen seconds of a 45 s item rendered whole minus the same stretch rendered
  from a cut with a 3.2 s lead-in, frames aligned; `dropped_seconds`, seconds of speech the whole
  render loses (20 dB or more under the reference) that the fresh render keeps (within 3 dB);
  `cold_start_db`, the first second of the cut against the same second inside the whole render.
  All are read on every 45 s item; the verdict is yes or no, never a score.
- `band_kept_db`: per-band output/reference energy over speech-active frames for 0–0.5, 0.5–2,
  2–4, 4–8, 8–12, 12–16 and 16–24 kHz; 0 dB preserved, negative dulled, positive invented.
- Fidelity is scored on `clean`, where there is nothing to remove, as harm someone could notice:
  the quiet tenth of the voice (`kept_coh_p10`), the level change (`level_err_db`, the absolute
  change per item, averaged), the error over 8–24 kHz (`hf_err_db`: per item, the mean absolute
  band error once the gain is undone, then averaged, so invented high band costs as much as lost
  high band and a dull item cannot cancel a bright one), `spk_sim` and NISQA, each
  against the untouched recording and each saturating where differences stop being audible; the
  spectrum (`mag_snr`, the signal-to-error ratio of the magnitude spectrogram after the best
  single gain, which does not read phase, from 10 to 30 dB); and the vocal sounds outside speech
  lost on clean recordings.
  Clean `si_sdr` is reported but not scored: it measures identity of the waveform, and a system
  that re-synthesizes a waveform its listener could not tell apart reads about 26 dB
  (`CONTROLS.md`) while the recognizers and quality predictors find nothing wrong and a listener
  hears no difference. The magnitude ratio
  saturates under what that re-synthesis reads, so it does not bring the objection back.
- `babble_supp_db`: energy of the residual (the output with its gain undone, minus the
  reference) over the residual the input started with, per item, averaged in dB; 0 dB is a
  passthrough, −10 dB ten decibels of din gone, positive an output further from clean than the
  input was. The ratio with the gain left in stays beside it (`babble_supp_raw_db`): a system
  that only turns everything down would read as suppression there and does not here.
- `nisqa_*`: NISQA v2 no-reference quality. Never read it alone: it rewards a clean-sounding
  rewrite even when the speaker is gone.
- Real recordings: `deleted_rate_pct` (VAD speech segments that collapse by more than 10 dB once
  the system's gain is undone, pooled over the real conditions and primary-speaker-anchored by
  `scorerealx.py`, so a removed background voice is not charged as a deletion),
  `speech_rms_delta_db`, `nonvoiced_supp_db` (the output's energy over the input's on the 20 ms
  frames at least 40 ms clear of the detected speech whose input is above −80 dBFS at the 16 kHz reading, the output's
  delay undone; gain undone; `nonvoiced_supp_raw_db` beside it),
  `dnsmos_ovr`.

Three corrections keep heterogeneous systems comparable, all reported: **level calibration** (the median output/input RMS over `clean`, undone in every kept, suppression, high-band and deletion reading, so an output gain can pose neither as preservation nor as suppression); **delay alignment** (best lag within ±100 ms before every reference metric); and a **band-limited twin** of every reference metric with both sides round-tripped through 16 kHz (`*_nb`), on which 16 kHz-native systems are ranked in their own tier.

- Diagnostic, on the real recordings: NISQA on the primary speaker's segments alone (`nisqa_mos_primary` in the NISQA sidecar), each segment of two seconds or more scored on its own and the item read as their duration-weighted mean, with the eligible duration and its share of the primary speaker's speech. The segment boundaries are the input's, from the deletion sidecar, applied identically to every output: an output never chooses the segments it is judged on. The quality axis reads the whole-clip value; this per-segment reading is reported beside it.

## Downstream ASR

The value of isolation to a listener is hard to put in one number; the value to a recognizer is not.
`scripts/scoreasr.py` transcribes every system's output of the conditions below with a popular open recognizer (Whisper large-v3 through faster-whisper, beam 5; Whisper small as a less robust second; the Inworld speech-to-text API as a hosted third) and scores the transcript against the item's golden text.
Golden texts are part of the set (`transcripts.jsonl`), one per target utterance: the corpus transcript confirmed word for word against the audio by Gemini 3.1 Pro (tier `corpus-verified`), or, where the source came with none, Gemini's verbatim transcript where Whisper large-v3 independently agrees within 10 % (tier `recognisers-agree`).
For the few recordings no second source confirms (proper nouns, numbers, a romanized corpus text, a whisper the recognizer misheard), Gemini's transcript, checked by ear, is kept as tier `gemini-accepted`; a text a person edits by ear is kept as `human-verified`.
Where the audio is in another language than the item's label says, the row names the one `spoken`, and the item is decoded and normalized in it (the manifest is part of the set's identity and stays as built).
An item without a golden text falls back to the recognizer's own transcript of the clean reference, and every sidecar records how many items each condition scored that way.
The `wild` rows are scored that way throughout: spontaneous speech, with its fillers, false starts and clipped words, has no single verbatim text two transcribers agree on, so those rows measure what a system changes in the recognizer's reading of the recording, not its distance from a transcript.
Both sides go through Whisper's text normalizer (the English one for English, the basic one otherwise); Chinese, Cantonese and Japanese are scored per character (a recognizer with no Cantonese model decodes it as written Chinese).
The ASR table is generated by `scripts/asrtable.py` from the sidecars; its last column is the offline Inworld Voice Isolator's change in error rate against the unprocessed input, in % of the input's.
Read it as where a front end helps or hurts a recognizer, not as a ranking: a robust recognizer needs little help above 0 dB, and every system that removes sound can remove words.
The same sidecars hold Whisper small (`--asr small`), which is less robust and gains more from every front end, and the Inworld recognizer (`--asr inworld/inworld-stt-1`, `INWORLD_API_KEY`).

The ASR table reports the error rate (%) of a recognizer's transcripts per condition, pooled, with the 95 % interval half-width; lower is better.

### Under a competing voice

Two things a recognizer can ask of an isolator when another voice is in the room, kept apart because they are opposite contracts: remove the chatter behind the speaker, or keep both sides of a conversation.
For a system that keeps one speaker, the conversation slice reads the cost of that choice.

`scripts/asrtable.py` writes the slices beside its main table: error rates pooled over each slice's items, one row per recognizer and unit, and the paired change against the unprocessed input with a 95 % interval that resamples the recordings and beds behind the items; a unit with fewer than 20 items in a slice is not shown.

### Content preservation

The preservation axis's retention metric (`kept_coh_p10`, which fidelity reads on `clean` too) reads 100 ms blocks through a percentile, and [CONTROLS.md](CONTROLS.md) shows what that cannot see: one dropped consonant, syllable or short word.
`scripts/scorecontent.py` is an ASR-based content diagnostic.
One recognizer (Whisper large-v3) transcribes every system's output, the transcript is aligned word by word to the item's verified transcript, and each reference word is counted as kept, substituted or deleted, with insertions counted separately, by kind of word: numbers, short words (three letters or fewer), consonant-final words and the rest.
The panel is every English item of the scored conditions with a verified transcript (the corpus text confirmed against the audio by a language model, or the model's own transcript accepted by ear; the caption gives the counts), so the reference is never a recognizer's unchecked guess, and every rate is read next to the unprocessed input's. The change column's interval resamples the recordings and beds behind the items; where it includes zero, the change is a favorable or unfavorable point estimate, not a resolved difference, and most rows are of that kind.
The `ctl_*` rows are the metric controls rendered onto the same panel (`--controls`): the reference untouched, with 20 to 320 ms of speech removed, re-synthesized, and with its top octave replaced by noise, so the table says what the check itself can and cannot see.
It cannot see a dropout under about 320 ms: a strong recognizer restores the word from context, which is why the block-level blind spot in CONTROLS.md still needs a signal-level, word-timed metric.

`scripts/scorecontent.py --markdown-out <file>` writes the table: words lost per hundred reference words of each kind, substitutions and insertions overall, and each system's change in deletions against the unprocessed input with a 95 % interval that resamples the recordings and beds behind the items.

## How a score is bound to what was rendered

Every rendering process writes a receipt beside its outputs before it renders (`wrappers/receipt.py`): the renderer's code and the package's, the weights, the installed packages, the device, the arguments and the inputs it renders.
The score files carry that identity and a digest of the outputs' bytes.
A system's probe and same-room pair renders must come from the environment that rendered its board row: the scorers refuse receipts of another one.
The scorers fingerprint their own code through its syntax tree (`observations.code_digest`), and `leaderboard.py` refuses to mix score files of different scorers, anchor versions or builds of the set.

`controls.py` scores synthetic systems built from the paired audio of a fixed slice (the reference untouched, louder, late, upside down, re-synthesized, with 20 to 320 ms removed, with its top octave cut or invented, silence, the bed alone) through the same scorer as a real system, holds every one to a declared expectation and writes [CONTROLS.md](CONTROLS.md).
